# The model

A one-dimensional water-quality model of the Ganga from Haridwar (km 0) to Patna (km 1,369), on 5 km cells with a one-day time step. Code: `ganga/model/core.py`. Skill numbers at the end of this file are generated by calibration (`data/curated/skill.json`).

## What it computes

Daily concentrations along the river of BOD, dissolved oxygen (DO), fecal coliform, ammonia-N, nitrate-N and total phosphorus, from:

- river flow at 40 points (GloFAS, via Open-Meteo), which also gives tributary inflows as the flow gained between points;
- air temperature, sunlight and rain at 26 places (ERA5 history, Open-Meteo forecast);
- sewage from 30 towns (`data/curated/loads.csv`) plus a diffuse load along the banks;
- water quality of the Ramganga, Kali, Yamuna, Gomti and Ghaghara at their mouths, taken from monitoring stations as a 12-month climatology.

## Transport

State is mass flux (flow × concentration) per cell. Each day, the water arriving at a cell is traced back one day along the flow:

    flux(i, t) = flux(source of i, t−1) · exp(−∫k dτ along the path)
               + Σ over loads entering along the path of  load · exp(−∫k dτ from its entry point)

Cells less than one day's travel from Haridwar take the upstream boundary instead. Loads and decay are integrated analytically along the path, so the scheme is exact for steady first-order problems and stable at any step size; a pulse moves at the water speed rather than appearing everywhere at once.

Velocity and depth follow power laws of flow: `u = u_ref · (Q/1000)^0.4`, `h = h_ref · (Q/1000)^0.4`.

## Kinetics (rates per day, corrected for water temperature T with θ^(T−20))

| Constituent | Loss | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| BOD | decay `kd` + settling `ks` | |
| Ammonia-N | nitrification `kn` + denitrification `kdn` | |
| Inorganic N (ammonia + nitrate) | `kdn` | nitrate = inorganic N − ammonia |
| Phosphorus | settling `kp` | |
| Fecal coliform | die-off `kfc` + sunlight term `kfc_light · solar / depth` | |
| DO deficit | reaeration `ka` (O'Connor–Dobbins, `3.93·u^0.5/h^1.5`) | sources: `kd·BOD + 4.57·kn·NH4 + SOD/h − photo·solar/h` |

DO = saturation − deficit; saturation from temperature (Benson–Krause) and altitude. Water temperature is a linear function of air temperature.

## Loads

- **Towns:** untreated flow × raw-sewage strength + treated flow × effluent strength, scaled by a global factor, a factor for each of the three big clusters (Kanpur, Prayagraj, Varanasi), and `exp(−distance from bank / 15 km)`.
- **Storms:** above a rain threshold, a share of treated flow bypasses treatment; street wash-off adds load in proportion to rain and population.
- **Diffuse:** a uniform load per km of river (villages, livestock, unsewered areas), increased by rain.
- **Change over time:** the inventory describes 2024. Two fitted factors (`load_2016`, `diffuse_2016`) set how much larger town and diffuse loads were in 2016, falling linearly to 2024. They stand in for sewerage and sanitation built since 2016; they are not a plant-by-plant account.
- **Tributaries:** each tributary enters where the flow model shows its gain, with observed monthly-climatology BOD, DO and coliform; ammonia, nitrogen and phosphorus scale with its BOD.

## Calibration

`python -m ganga.cli calibrate` fits 25 parameters (rates, hydraulics, sewage strengths, load factors) with differential evolution. The score is, summed over DO, BOD and log10(coliform), mean squared error divided by observed variance, plus a lightly weighted nitrate term from yearly ranges.

- **Used for fitting:** monthly and dated measurements before 2023 at four of every five main-stem stations.
- **Held out:** everything from 2023 on, and every fifth station entirely.

## Uncertainty

A forecast is an ensemble: the best-scoring parameter sets from calibration × river-flow forecast members. To that spread is added the residual scatter seen in calibration (model error plus sampling noise in a single monthly grab sample). The published band is the 5th–95th percentile of the result, and the bathing-limit probabilities are the share of the ensemble outside each limit.

In the dry season the flow members are almost identical, so nearly all of the band comes from parameters and residual scatter.

## Verified against closed-form solutions (`tests/test_model.py`)

- first-order decay: matches `exp(−k·t)` to 1e-6;
- Streeter–Phelps oxygen sag: within 3% of the sag depth, minimum at the right place;
- dilution at a tributary: exact;
- conservative tracer: mass in = mass out within 0.1%;
- a one-day spill peaks downstream after the travel time, with nothing arriving early;
- uniform diffuse load reaches the analytical equilibrium within 2%.

## Known weaknesses

- **Flow is modelled, not gauged**, and ignores canal withdrawals at Haridwar, Bijnor and Narora: dry-season flow above Kanpur is probably too high, which the calibration partly absorbs into load factors.
- **Loads are coarse:** official "utilised treatment" figures understate what reaches the river; fitted multipliers correct the total but not individual drains. Industrial effluent (e.g. tanneries) is not separate from sewage.
- **Nitrate, ammonia, phosphorus:** no monthly observations; nitrate is loosely constrained by yearly ranges, the other two not at all.
- **Confluence positions** follow the flow model and can sit a few tens of km from the true ones.
- **Above Bijnor and below Buxar** there are few measurements; Patna's sewage mostly enters below the model's end.
- **Observations are monthly grab samples** with large scatter (coliform especially); part of the "error" is in the measurements.
- **Unreported spills** cannot be forecast; the scenario tool shows their effect if they are entered.

**Reading this honestly**

- On held-out data the model is about level with a no-model benchmark that predicts each month from the station's own past average: better for BOD in later years (0.98 vs 1.10) and for coliform at unseen stations (0.47 vs 0.57), worse for DO (1.01 vs 0.92 and 0.91 vs 0.83), and level elsewhere.
- NSE is low (DO about 0.3–0.4, coliform 0.1–0.2, BOD slightly negative): the model explains the broad along-river and seasonal pattern but little of the month-to-month scatter of single grab samples. BOD is biased low by about 0.5 mg/L.
- The 90% band holds 93–97% of held-out measurements, so it is slightly wide rather than overconfident.
- Worst spot: just below Kanpur, where measured coliform (median 43,000 MPN/100 mL in 2023–26) is far above the model (about 5,000). The big-city hotspots are under-predicted.
- What the model adds over past averages is not accuracy at monitored stations but coverage of unmonitored places, response to flow and rain, forecasts with ranges, and what-if runs.
